# Prior Art on Intercept-Fitting Strategies in Coordinate Descent for Regularized GLMs

## TL;DR
- **No published paper explicitly compares the four intercept-fitting strategies (gradient / single Newton / iterate-to-convergence / damped Newton with line search) inside direct-Newton coordinate descent (CD) solvers for regularized GLMs.** All prior CD-for-GLM work reviewed either (a) uses IRLS, where the intercept update collapses to closed-form inside the quadratic surrogate, or (b) treats the intercept as "just another coordinate" with the penalty multiplier set to zero, with no separate analysis.
- **The closest prior remarks are oblique**: glmnet (Tay, Narasimhan & Hastie 2023, JSS 106:1) uses *step-size halving* on the joint (β₀, β) update; newGLMNET (Yuan, Ho & Lin 2012, JMLR 13:1999–2030) applies an Armijo line search to the *joint* Newton direction; adelie (Yang & Hastie 2024, arXiv:2405.08631) **explicitly declines line search** and reports that Friedman et al.'s extra inner minimization for multinomial identifiability "resulted in a less stable algorithm often diverging." None of these isolates the intercept.
- **The gap you describe is real.** Your paper appears novel: no theoretical analysis specific to intercept updates in CD, no empirical benchmark of intercept-update schemes, and the terms "convergence updates" / "inner intercept loop" do not appear in this literature. Proceed — but position the paper carefully against the IRLS-based mainline (where the issue vanishes) and against newGLMNET's joint line search.

## Key Findings

**1. The dominant paradigm is IRLS, where the intercept question disappears.** The glmnet line of work — Friedman, Hastie & Tibshirani (2010, "Regularization Paths for Generalized Linear Models via Coordinate Descent", *Journal of Statistical Software* 33(1):1–22); Simon, Friedman, Hastie & Tibshirani (2011, JSS 39(5)) for Cox; Tay, Narasimhan & Hastie (2023, "Elastic Net Regularization Paths for All Generalized Linear Models", JSS 106(1):1–31) — together with ncvreg (Breheny & Huang 2011), the H2O GLM solver, glum, and ExclusiveLasso, all use an outer Newton / IRLS loop that builds a penalized weighted-least-squares (PWLS) quadratic surrogate, then runs CD on the surrogate. In the PWLS sub-problem the intercept update is a closed-form weighted mean of the working residual — so gradient, Newton, "to convergence," and damped Newton all coincide. Tay et al. (2023, §2.3) are explicit: *"As written, Algorithm 1 is a proximal Newton algorithm with a constant step size of 1, and hence it may not converge in certain cases. To ensure convergence, we can implement step-size halving … (with a similar formula for the intercept)."* This is the closest the glmnet literature gets to discussing intercept-specific behavior, and even there the halving is applied to the joint update.

**2. Direct-Newton CD papers treat the intercept as a regular coordinate with λ = 0.** newGLMNET / CDN (Yuan, Ho & Lin 2012, JMLR 13:1999–2030) is the canonical direct-Newton CD paper for L1-logistic. They state (§6, p. 2012): *"All these methods can be easily extended to solve logistic regression with a bias term b… Because the GLMNET implementation solves [the biased problem] instead of [the unbiased problem], in our comparison, [the biased problem] is used."* That is the *only* substantive mention of the bias term in the paper. Their algorithms (CDN Algorithm 1; newGLMNET Algorithms 3–4) make no distinction between bias and feature coordinates: CDN applies the same 1-D Newton step + Armijo line search formula uniformly, and newGLMNET runs one joint Armijo line search per outer iteration. The semismooth Newton CD method of Yi & Huang (2017, hqreg, arXiv:1509.02957) and Breheny & Huang's ncvreg logistic CD do the same — Breheny & Huang (2011, §3) say it bluntly: *"step 2 must now involve the updating of the intercept term, which may be accomplished without modification of the underlying framework by setting λ = 0 for the intercept term."*

**3. adelie (Yang & Hastie 2024) is the most recent direct-Newton CD paper, and it folds the intercept into the joint quasi-Newton step.** Their Algorithm 4 for general convex losses (Eq. 21, pp. 13–14) updates (β₀, β) *jointly* by solving a Gaussian-loss group-elastic-net sub-problem in which β₀ enters as an unpenalized scalar; the inner block-CD then sees the intercept as one block. They explicitly say (§4): *"as in Friedman et al. [2010], we do not perform the line search since it will significantly impact the overall runtime and it does not seem necessary in practice thanks to the warm-starts in our pathwise solver. We observe fast convergence, usually only requiring 1 to 5 iterations."* For multinomial they note (§5) that Friedman et al. (2010) performed an extra inner minimization of the penalty with respect to (d₀, d) — the only "convergence update"-like idea I found in print — and Yang & Hastie report *"we also experimented with minimizing the penalty with respect to d₀ and d, but to our surprise, this resulted in a less stable algorithm often diverging."* This is the closest published comparison to one of your four strategies, but it is multinomial-identifiability-specific, not a clean head-to-head.

**4. The wider direct-Newton / proximal-Newton CD literature is silent on the intercept.** I checked: Tseng & Yun (2009, "block coordinate gradient descent" / CGD); Lee, Sun & Saunders (2014, proximal Newton); Bian, Li, Liu & Yang (2019, Parallel CDN, IEEE TPAMI); Hsieh, Sustik, Dhillon & Ravikumar (QUIC, graphical lasso); Trofimov & Genkin (d-GLMNET, 2014/2016); Massias et al. (celer, ICML 2018); Bertrand & Massias (Anderson acceleration of CD, AISTATS 2021); Bertrand et al. (skglm, NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2204.07826); Fercoq (2019 generic CD solver, arXiv:1812.00628); Karimireddy et al. (greedy CD for composite problems, 2018); Johnson & Guestrin (Blitz); Fercoq & Richtárik (accelerated CD); Wu & Lange (2008, *Annals of Applied Statistics* 2(1):224–244); Genkin, Lewis & Madigan (BBR, *Technometrics* 49(3):291–304, 2007); Tibshirani et al. (strong rules, JRSSB 2012); Zeng, Yang & Breheny (hybrid safe-strong rules); Michoel (2014, natural CD for GLMs, arXiv:1405.4225); and the ECCD paper of Wang, Shao, Jiang & Devarakonda (2025, "Enhanced Cyclic Coordinate Descent Methods for Elastic Net Penalized Linear Models," NeurIPS 2025 poster, arXiv:2510.19999, published 22 Oct 2025). None of these singles out the intercept update, compares intercept-update strategies, or provides theoretical convergence rates specific to the intercept coordinate in CD on the GLM objective.

**5. The terms "convergence updates" and "inner intercept loop" do not appear in this literature** in any sense matching your usage. "Inner loop" in glmnet-style references the CD pass on the IRLS quadratic surrogate, not an intercept-specific iteration.

## Details

**Direct-Newton vs. IRLS framing.** Your distinction is correct and important: in IRLS the per-coordinate sub-problem on the surrogate is a 1-D weighted-least-squares problem with a closed-form solution, so the intercept update is unambiguous and the four strategies coincide. In direct-Newton CD (where each coordinate update is a 1-D Newton step on the *true* GLM negative log-likelihood, sometimes called CDN — Yuan, Ho & Lin 2012), the per-coordinate sub-problem is a 1-D nonquadratic minimization, and the update can be (a) a single gradient step, (b) a single Newton step, (c) iterated until 1-D convergence, or (d) a Newton step with line-search / step-halving / trust-region damping. The literature uses (b) or (d) uniformly for all coordinates including the intercept, with the implicit assumption that the intercept does not need special treatment. There is no published recognition that the intercept is qualitatively different (high curvature, unpenalized, often the dominant direction at high λ where most β_j are zero).

**Closest comparable analyses.**
- **newGLMNET / CDN (Yuan, Ho & Lin 2012)**: uses Newton + Armijo line search on every coordinate (CDN) or one joint Armijo line search per outer iteration (newGLMNET). Convergence is proved via Tseng & Yun (2009). The intercept is just a coordinate with λ = 0 in CDN; in newGLMNET it is part of the joint direction. The paper reports that *"In Figure 1(f), to reach the horizontal dotted line, newGLMNET is ten times faster than CDN"* on the dense *epsilon* dataset — but this comparison is global, not intercept-specific.
- **Parallel CDN (Bian, Li, Liu & Yang, IEEE TPAMI 2019)**: extends CDN with P-dimensional line search. Same treatment of bias as in CDN.
- **Trofimov & Genkin d-GLMNET (2014, 2016)**: distributed CD with a global line search on the combined direction. No special intercept handling.
- **adelie (Yang & Hastie 2024)**: explicitly discards line search and uses constant step size 1 on the joint (β₀, β) Newton step (Algorithm 4). The empirical observation "usually 1 to 5 iterations" is their entire convergence story; they note (§5) that an extra inner minimization on (d₀, d) for multinomial diverged.
- **glmnet / Tay et al. (2023, §2.3)**: step-size halving on the joint update is offered as an option, "with a similar formula for the intercept." But the four strategies coincide here because the inner sub-problem is the PWLS surrogate, not the true GLM.

**Partial comparisons that *do* exist.** None compares strategies 1–4 for the intercept specifically. The only comparisons in print are: (i) joint Newton vs. joint Newton-with-line-search on the full coefficient vector (newGLMNET vs. CDN; adelie vs. glmnet's step-halving); (ii) IRLS-CD vs. direct-CDN globally (Yuan, Ho & Lin 2012, Figures 1–3); (iii) Yang & Hastie's brief negative remark on the multinomial inner penalty minimization.

**Theoretical convergence analysis specific to intercept updates: none found.** General CD convergence theory — Tseng (2001, *Journal of Optimization Theory and Applications* 109(3):475–494); Tseng & Yun (2009, *Mathematical Programming* 117(1–2):387–423); Beck & Tetruashvili (2013, "On the Convergence of Block Coordinate Descent Type Methods," *SIAM Journal on Optimization* 23(4):2037–2060, DOI 10.1137/120887679); Nutini, Schmidt, Laradji, Friedlander & Koepke (2015, "Coordinate Descent Converges Faster with the Gauss-Southwell Rule Than Random Selection," ICML 2015, PMLR 37:1632–1641) — covers block-CD with arbitrary unpenalized blocks but does not characterize the intercept's convergence behavior separately. There is no paper of the form "intercept-update step-size analysis in direct-Newton CD."

**Software documentation.** glmnet, sklearn, liblinear, celer, skglm, adelie, ncvreg, hqreg, biglasso, glum, H2O GLM — none of their reference papers contains a comparison of intercept-update strategies. (glum's documentation does carefully describe their IRLS-CD framework citing newGLMNET, but no intercept-specific comparison.) The dallascard/lrb GitHub note *"Note that at the moment, the intercept is being regularized (as is the case in liblinear and scikit-learn)"* reflects how rarely this design choice is even surfaced.

## Recommendations

1. **Publish.** Frame the paper as the first systematic comparison of intercept-update strategies in direct-Newton CD for regularized GLMs. The contribution is real and the literature gap is unambiguous.

2. **Position carefully** against three reference points:
   - **vs. glmnet / IRLS**: explain that the issue is invisible under IRLS because the quadratic surrogate makes the intercept update closed-form; your contribution is for direct-Newton CD where this no longer holds.
   - **vs. newGLMNET / CDN**: their joint Armijo line search is roughly equivalent to your damped-Newton-on-everything strategy; you should benchmark against the "damp the whole direction, intercept included" baseline.
   - **vs. adelie**: their constant-step-size joint Newton (no line search) is roughly your strategy 2 applied jointly; benchmark against this too.

3. **Empirical benchmarks should include**:
   - Logistic regression (binary classification, separable and non-separable data).
   - Poisson regression with very different sample means (where intercept conditioning matters most because exp(β₀) dominates the linear predictor at high λ).
   - Cox regression (no intercept — useful negative control / robustness check; note that the Cox partial likelihood absorbs any constant).
   - Multinomial regression (where Friedman et al.'s identifiability move and Yang & Hastie's divergence observation create a natural connection to "convergence updates").
   - Compare against glmnet (IRLS), adelie (joint Newton, no line search), newGLMNET/liblinear (joint Newton with line search), skglm/celer.

4. **Theoretical contribution**: even a simple result — e.g., that running the intercept update to convergence between feature sweeps preserves the global convergence guarantees of Tseng & Yun (2009) under standard regularity, and gives a measurable per-outer-iteration improvement on the intercept residual — would be novel. The Beck & Tetruashvili (2013) block-CD framework is a natural vehicle.

5. **Cite explicitly** the four loci in the literature that *almost* address your question and that reviewers will expect to see acknowledged: Yang & Hastie (2024) §5 multinomial remark; Tay et al. (2023) §2.3 step-halving; Yuan, Ho & Lin (2012) §6 bias-term Eq. 33; Breheny & Huang (2011) §3 logistic regression intercept-as-λ=0 remark.

6. **Benchmarks that would change the recommendation**: if your "convergence update" strategy (3) shows divergence on multinomial — matching Yang & Hastie's observation — that would be an important caveat; if damped Newton (4) shows no advantage over plain Newton (2) on logistic/Poisson with warm starts along the regularization path, then the paper's framing should emphasize Poisson and ill-conditioned regimes (intercept-dominated, small minimum-mean-count) rather than logistic.

## Caveats

- I could not retrieve the full text of Yi & Huang (2017, hqreg/SNCD, arXiv:1509.02957) due to rate-limiting; the abstract and what I could see confirm the intercept is just another coordinate, but a complete algorithm-box verification is owed.
- I did not inspect the C++ source code of adelie/glmnet/skglm; implementation may differ slightly from the published algorithm boxes (e.g., an undocumented inner iteration on β₀). Worth checking the GitHub source of adelie before claiming complete novelty, since at the source level the inner solver may iterate the unpenalized block to higher precision than the algorithm box suggests.
- The hqreg/SNCD work targets Huber/quantile loss, not the canonical GLM losses you list, so it is only tangentially relevant.
- Some niche workshop papers, theses, and technical reports may discuss this; I did not find any, but the search space is large. A focused arXiv and Google Scholar search closer to publication time is prudent, especially with terms like "unpenalized block" + "coordinate descent" + GLM.
- Your strategy (3) "convergence updates" is conceptually close to *block coordinate minimization* applied to {intercept} as one block and {features} as another — there is a large literature on block CD (Tseng 2001; Tseng & Yun 2009; Beck & Tetruashvili 2013), but none of it identifies the intercept as the distinguished block of interest in regularized GLMs. You may want to position (3) as "exact block minimization of the unpenalized block" to connect to this literature without overclaiming novelty of the abstract idea.
- glmnet, ncvreg, and adelie standardize features before fitting, which improves intercept conditioning. Your benchmarks should test both standardized and unstandardized inputs because the relative ranking of strategies 1–4 likely depends on it.
