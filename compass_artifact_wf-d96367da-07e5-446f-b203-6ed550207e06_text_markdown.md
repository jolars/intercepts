# IRLS vs. Direct Newton inside Coordinate Descent for Regularized GLMs: A Literature Review

## TL;DR
- The literature reaches a clear consensus that for ℓ1/elastic-net penalized GLMs (logistic, Poisson, multinomial, Cox), the dominant approach is a **two-level "outer Newton/IRLS, inner CD"** scheme — the GLMNET pattern of Friedman, Hastie, and Tibshirani (JSS 2010) — rather than a one-level "direct Newton on each coordinate" scheme such as LIBLINEAR's CDN; this is because exp/log evaluations dominate the per-iteration cost of direct CD-Newton on logistic/Poisson losses, while a quadratic surrogate amortizes them across one entire inner CD pass.
- The seminal CDN-vs-GLMNET debate was settled by Yuan, Ho & Lin (JMLR 2012, "newGLMNET"), which kept the IRLS-style outer/inner split but added a regularized Hessian (νI), Armijo line search, adaptive inner tolerance, and two-level shrinking — recovering Tseng–Yun-style global convergence and local linear/quadratic rates that the original GLMNET lacked, and reporting up to ~10× speedups over CDN on dense logistic problems where exp/log dominates.
- For numerical stability — separated/quasi-separated logistic data, near-zero IRLS weights w_i = π̂_i(1 − π̂_i), and ill-conditioning — the practical guidance from glmnet authors and successors (Tay–Narasimhan–Hastie JSS 2023; Yang–Hastie 2024 "adelie") is (i) include even a tiny ℓ2 component, (ii) bound the working response z, (iii) use warm-started path-following with strong rules, and (iv) regularize the inner Hessian; recent solvers (celer, picasso, biglasso, snpnet, adelie, ECCD) preserve the outer-quadratic / inner-CD architecture and compete on screening, working sets, dual extrapolation, and memory layout rather than on replacing IRLS with a direct CD-Newton.

## Key Findings

**The two competing architectures.** All major regularized-GLM solvers fit one of two templates:

1. **Outer IRLS / inner CD on a quadratic surrogate ("proximal Newton with CD subsolver").** At iterate w_k, form the IRLS quadratic q_k(d) = ∇L(w_k)ᵀd + ½ dᵀ ∇²L(w_k) d + λ‖w_k+d‖₁ − λ‖w_k‖₁ — equivalently a penalized weighted least squares with weights w_i and working response z_i — then solve q_k by cyclic CD with closed-form soft-thresholding. This is GLMNET (Friedman et al. 2010), Cox-glmnet (Simon et al. 2011), the all-GLM extension of glmnet (Tay et al. 2023), newGLMNET (Yuan et al. 2012), ncvreg (Breheny & Huang 2011), picasso (Ge et al. JMLR 2019), biglasso (Zeng & Breheny 2021), snpnet (Qian et al. 2020), adelie/grpnet (Yang & Hastie 2024), ECCD (Wang et al. NeurIPS 2025).

2. **Direct one-level CD with per-coordinate Newton step on the original GLM loss ("CDN").** At each coordinate j, take a 1-D Newton-soft-threshold step on L(w) + λ|w_j| with line search, evaluating exp/log every step. This is Yuan, Chang, Hsieh & Lin (JMLR 2010)'s CDN (the LIBLINEAR L1-LR solver), used as a baseline by Yuan et al. 2012, Massias et al. 2018/2020, and Benchopt 2022.

**Empirical winners.** For *logistic regression on dense data* (e.g., epsilon, gisette, webspam) the IRLS/inner-CD architecture wins. Yuan, Ho & Lin (2012, Fig. 1(f), p. 2021) state verbatim: "to reach the horizontal dotted line, newGLMNET is ten times faster than CDN" on the epsilon dataset (Pascal Large Scale Learning Challenge 2008: 400,000 training samples × 2,000 features). Their Table 1 ("Timing analysis of the first CD cycle of CDN. Time is in seconds.") attributes this to exp/log: CDN spends 64.25 s out of 88.18 s (73.0%) in exp/log on epsilon and 72.89 s out of 109.39 s (66.6%) in exp/log on webspam, while newGLMNET spends ≤1% (the inner CD operates on a quadratic with no exp/log; exp/log is incurred only at outer iterations and is therefore amortized from O(nℓ) to O(ℓ) per cycle). For *cheap losses (L2-loss SVM)* CDN wins, confirming the IRLS architecture's advantage is loss-cost-driven. For *sparse document data* the original Yuan et al. (2010) JMLR comparison concluded CDN narrowly beat the original GLMNET, but the 2012 newGLMNET paper closes that gap and surpasses CDN even on sparse rcv1, news20, and yahoo-korea.

**Convergence theory.** The GLMNET paper itself does not prove convergence beyond invoking Tseng (2001). newGLMNET fills that gap: by adding νI (ν=10⁻¹²) to the Hessian and adopting an Armijo-type line search satisfying f(w_k + λd) − f(w_k) ≤ σλ(∇L(w_k)ᵀd + γdᵀH_kd + ‖w_k+d‖₁ − ‖w_k‖₁), it satisfies the conditions of Tseng & Yun (2009, Theorems 1(e) and 3) and so achieves (i) global subsequential convergence, (ii) at-least-linear local rate when L is strictly convex (XᵀX > 0), and (iii) **local quadratic** rate when the exact Hessian is used and the subproblem is solved exactly. Lee, Sun & Saunders (SIAM J. Optim. 2014, "Proximal Newton-type methods for minimizing composite functions") give the abstract framework for this class — including inexact subproblem solves — proving superlinear convergence under strong convexity and an inexactness criterion. Tseng (JOTA 2001) and Tseng & Yun (Math. Program. 2009) supply the foundational coordinate-descent convergence machinery; Wright (Math. Program. 2015, "Coordinate descent algorithms") is the standard modern survey.

**Numerical stability and separated data.** The IRLS approximation is degenerate when fitted probabilities π̂_i go to 0 or 1, because the working weights w_i = π̂_i(1−π̂_i) collapse and the working response z_i diverges — the same pathology as classical separation in unpenalized logistic regression (Albert & Anderson 1984). glmnet's documentation and the original Friedman–Hastie–Tibshirani paper recommend a small ℓ2 component to "remove degeneracies and wild behavior." Truncating the weights from below (w_i ← max(w_i, ε)) and the response (clip z_i) is standard. Yuan, Ho & Lin (2012) document that the original GLMNET "failed to train some large-scale data" and "failed to solve two large problems" in the Yuan et al. 2010 comparison "because of either memory problems or lengthy running time" — notably KDD2010-b, webspam, epsilon, and yahoo-korea — issues newGLMNET fixes via Hessian regularization, line search, and adaptive inner tolerance. In the regularized setting, the ℓ1 (or elastic-net) penalty itself fixes the *existence-of-MLE* aspect of separation (the penalized objective is bounded below and coercive when α<1 or the active set yields a strictly convex restricted problem), but it does *not* fix the IRLS *numerical* failure mode — that requires explicit weight floors or a direct CD-Newton step that does not invert the diagonal.

**Recent (2020–2026) developments.** Massias, Vaiter, Gramfort & Salmon (JMLR 2020, "Dual extrapolation for sparse GLMs"; companion ICML 2018 "celer") establish a Vector-AutoRegressive structure of dual iterates after sign identification and use it to construct working sets and tighter duality-gap stopping criteria — the celer package documentation reports it tackles "large-scale datasets with millions of features **up to 100 times faster than scikit-learn**" while preserving the inner architecture. Tay–Narasimhan–Hastie (JSS 2023) extend glmnet to arbitrary GLM families and to Cox with (start,stop] and strata using exactly the IRLS-CD pattern. Qian et al. (PLOS Genet. 2020, snpnet) scale this to UK Biobank-size genotype matrices via a screening "BASIL" wrapper around glmnet. Yang & Hastie (arXiv 2024, "adelie") generalize to group lasso/elastic net, solving each block-coordinate update with **Newton's method plus adaptive bisection**, giving quadratic convergence per block; verbatim from the abstract: "Our benchmarks show that our package adelie performs 3 to 10 times faster than the next fastest package on a wide array of both simulated and real datasets." Wang, Shao, Jiang & Devarakonda (NeurIPS 2025, "ECCD") propose unrolling the cyclic-CD recurrences via a Taylor expansion to amortize nonlinear (exp/log) ops in batches, claiming ~3× average speedup over glmnet across regularization-path benchmarks while explicitly preserving IRLS stability ("avoids the convergence delay and numerical instability exhibited by block coordinate descent").

**Alternatives and direct competitors.** Beyond CD-based methods, four families compete: (i) FISTA/proximal-gradient (Beck–Teboulle 2009; used in scikit-learn's `saga` for elastic-net logistic); (ii) ADMM (Boyd et al.; SnapVX, Hallac et al. JMLR 2017); (iii) interior-point (Koh, Kim & Boyd 2007); and (iv) **semismooth Newton augmented Lagrangian (SSNAL)** (Li, Sun & Toh 2018) — for difficult Lasso problems SSNAL achieves asymptotic superlinear convergence by exploiting second-order sparsity of the proximal map and is reported to dominate first-order solvers on hard real-data Lassos, though SSNAL has not displaced glmnet for routine path-following on GLMs. Benchopt (Moreau et al. NeurIPS 2022) provides the most reproducible recent comparison and concludes "the best algorithm largely depends on the dataset properties (size, sparsity), the hyperparameters, as well as hardware."

## Details

### Foundational / seminal references

- **Friedman, J. H., Hastie, T., Tibshirani, R. (2010).** "Regularization Paths for Generalized Linear Models via Coordinate Descent." *Journal of Statistical Software* 33(1):1–22. doi:10.18637/jss.v033.i01. — Establishes the outer-IRLS / inner-CD architecture (their Section 3 derives the penalized weighted-least-squares quadratic from the second-order Taylor expansion of the log-likelihood). Empirically dominates LARS and `glmpath` for paths over 100 λ values. Recommends adding a small ℓ2 to "remove degeneracies and wild behavior." Does not prove convergence of the two-level scheme but invokes Tseng (2001) on the inner CD.
- **Simon, N., Friedman, J., Hastie, T., Tibshirani, R. (2011).** "Regularization Paths for Cox's Proportional Hazards Model via Coordinate Descent." *J. Stat. Software* 39(5):1–13. — Same pattern for Cox: forms a one-step Newton quadratic in the linear predictor η using the partial-likelihood gradient and Hessian, replaces the full Hessian by its diagonal w_i (justified by Hastie–Tibshirani 1990 GAM argument), then runs cyclic CD. Confirms warm-started path-following stabilizes Cox lasso even when p ≈ n.
- **Friedman, J., Hastie, T., Höfling, H., Tibshirani, R. (2007).** "Pathwise coordinate optimization." *Annals of Applied Statistics* 1(2):302–332. — Pre-cursor that introduces pathwise CD for the lasso and shows it beats LARS in many regimes.
- **Tseng, P. (2001).** "Convergence of a Block Coordinate Descent Method for Nondifferentiable Minimization." *J. Optim. Theory Appl.* 109(3):475–494. — Subsequence convergence of CD for f(x) = smooth + separable nonsmooth. Cited by virtually every CD-for-lasso paper.
- **Tseng, P., Yun, S. (2009).** "A coordinate gradient descent method for nonsmooth separable minimization." *Math. Program.* 117:387–423. — Provides the global convergence + linear-rate-under-error-bound theory used by newGLMNET.
- **Lee, J. D., Sun, Y., Saunders, M. A. (2014).** "Proximal Newton-Type Methods for Minimizing Composite Functions." *SIAM Journal on Optimization* 24(3):1420–1443. (NeurIPS 2012 conference version.) — The general framework: any composite f = g + h with g smooth strongly convex, h prox-friendly admits a proximal Newton iteration that achieves quadratic convergence with exact Hessian and superlinear with quasi-Newton or inexact subproblem solves under a forcing-sequence criterion. GLMNET is a special case.
- **Yuan, G.-X., Chang, K.-W., Hsieh, C.-J., Lin, C.-J. (2010).** "A Comparison of Optimization Methods and Software for Large-scale L1-regularized Linear Classification." *JMLR* 11:3183–3234. — Benchmarks BBR, CDN, SCD, CGD, IPM, BMRM, OWL-QN, GLMNET, TRON, Lassplore on rcv1, yahoo-japan/korea, news20, real-sim, a9a. Bottom line: "carefully implemented coordinate descent methods are very suitable for training large document data" — CDN best for sparse document data; TRON (trust-region Newton) best for the smaller-p, larger-n a9a; GLMNET fails on the largest problems due to memory or runtime.
- **Yuan, G.-X., Ho, C.-H., Lin, C.-J. (2012).** "An Improved GLMNET for L1-regularized Logistic Regression." *JMLR* 13:1999–2030. (KDD 2011 short version.) — The pivotal IRLS-vs-direct-Newton paper. Diagnoses why CDN is slow for logistic but fast for SVM (per-CD-step exp/log dominates: their Table 1 reports 66–73% time in exp/log for the first CD cycle on six datasets). Diagnoses why GLMNET fails on large data (no line search, equal inner/outer tolerance, no Hessian regularization). Fixes: regularize Hessian with νI=10⁻¹²I, Armijo line search (σ=0.01, β=0.5, γ=0; λ=1 always succeeds in their experiments), adaptive inner tolerance ε_in ← ε_in/4 if one inner cycle suffices, two-level shrinking, random permutation. Proves global subsequential convergence via Tseng–Yun (2009), local linear rate under strict convexity, local quadratic rate with exact Hessian and exact subproblem solve (Appendix B). Reports 10× speedup over CDN on epsilon; uniform speedup on all eight LIBSVM-scale benchmarks (KDD2010-b, rcv1, yahoo-japan, yahoo-korea, news20, epsilon, webspam, gisette).
- **Breheny, P., Huang, J. (2011).** "Coordinate descent algorithms for nonconvex penalized regression, with applications to biological feature selection." *Annals of Applied Statistics* 5(1):232–253. — Extends the IRLS/CD pattern to MCP and SCAD nonconvex penalties (`ncvreg`). Introduces convexity diagnostics for the locally convex region of the path.
- **Tibshirani, R., Bien, J., Friedman, J., Hastie, T., Simon, N., Taylor, J., Tibshirani, R. J. (2012).** "Strong rules for discarding predictors in lasso-type problems." *J. R. Stat. Soc. B* 74(2):245–266. — The screening rule that all modern CD-for-GLM solvers use.

### Recent (2018–2026) work

- **Massias, M., Gramfort, A., Salmon, J. (2018).** "Celer: a Fast Solver for the Lasso with Dual Extrapolation." *ICML*, PMLR 80:3321–3330. — Working-set + Gap-Safe screening + dual extrapolation; the celer documentation reports up to 100× over scikit-learn on lasso "with millions of features."
- **Massias, M., Vaiter, S., Gramfort, A., Salmon, J. (2020).** "Dual Extrapolation for Sparse GLMs." *JMLR* 21(234):1–33. — Generalizes dual extrapolation to all sparse GLMs (logistic, Poisson, …), proves a Vector-AutoRegressive structure of dual iterates after sign identification when the primal is solved by proximal gradient or cyclic CD.
- **Zhao, T., Liu, H., Zhang, T. (2018).** "Pathwise coordinate optimization for sparse learning: Algorithm and theory." *Annals of Statistics* 46(1):180–218. — Theoretical guarantees of global linear convergence for the Friedman et al. pathwise CD framework (and PICASSO for nonconvex).
- **Ge, J., Li, X., Jiang, H., Liu, H., Zhang, T., Wang, M., Zhao, T. (2019).** "Picasso: A Sparse Learning Library for High-Dimensional Data Analysis in R and Python." *JMLR* 20(44):1–5. — Pathwise CD for sparse linear, logistic, Poisson, scaled-lasso. Reports better performance than ncvreg on logistic with nonconvex regularizers, and stable performance where ncvreg "may converge very slow or fail to converge … especially when the tuning parameters are relatively small."
- **Zeng, Y., Breheny, P. (2021).** "The biglasso Package: A Memory- and Computation-Efficient Solver for Lasso Model Fitting with Big Data in R." *R Journal* 12(2):6–19. — Memory-mapped pathwise CD, OpenMP, hybrid+adaptive screening. The published paper reports "biglasso was fastest at obtaining solutions, providing 2× to 3.8× speedup compared to glmnet and ncvreg, and 2× to 4.6× speedup compared to picasso," and approximately 1.5× over glmnet for logistic regression specifically.
- **Tay, J. K., Narasimhan, B., Hastie, T. (2023).** "Elastic Net Regularization Paths for All Generalized Linear Models." *J. Stat. Software* 106(1):1–31. — glmnet 4.1; arbitrary GLM families via a `family` argument that calls `glm`'s IRLS internally for the quadratic, plus Cox with (start, stop] and strata. Discusses the inner-loop weighted-norm convergence criterion and the Newton-step weights for non-Gaussian families.
- **Qian, J., Tanigawa, Y., Du, W., Aguirre, M., Chang, C., Tibshirani, R., Rivas, M. A., Hastie, T. (2020).** "A fast and scalable framework for large-scale and ultrahigh-dimensional sparse regression with application to the UK Biobank." *PLOS Genetics* 16(10):e1009141. — `snpnet` extends glmnet via the BASIL screening loop for SNP matrices (~500K variants × 337K samples) for lasso/elastic-net linear, logistic, Cox.
- **Yang, J., Hastie, T. (2024).** "A Fast and Scalable Pathwise-Solver for Group Lasso and Elastic Net Penalized Regression via Block-Coordinate Descent." arXiv:2405.08631. — `adelie`: each block update solved by Newton's method + adaptive bisection (quadratic convergence). Authors report "adelie performs 3 to 10 times faster than the next fastest package on a wide array of both simulated and real datasets."
- **Wang, Y., Shao, Z., Jiang, T., Devarakonda, A. (2025).** "Enhanced Cyclic Coordinate Descent Methods for Elastic Net Penalized Linear Models." *NeurIPS 2025*. arXiv:2510.19999. — ECCD unrolls the CD recurrence for s steps via a local Taylor expansion to batch nonlinear ops. Claims ~3× over glmnet on benchmark logistic problems while explicitly avoiding "the convergence delay and numerical instability exhibited by block coordinate descent" — i.e., positions itself between cyclic CD and BCD.
- **Li, X., Sun, D., Toh, K.-C. (2018).** "A Highly Efficient Semismooth Newton Augmented Lagrangian Method for Solving Lasso Problems." *SIAM Journal on Optimization* 28(1):433–458. — SSNAL exploits second-order sparsity of the proximal map; asymptotic superlinear convergence; outperforms first-order solvers on hard real-data Lassos. Not yet a standard tool for GLMs but increasingly cited as the strongest non-CD competitor.
- **Hallac, D., Wong, C., Diamond, S., Sharang, A., Sosič, R., Boyd, S., Leskovec, J. (2017).** "SnapVX: A Network-Based Convex Optimization Solver." *JMLR* 18(4):1–5. — ADMM-based reference for network-structured GLM problems.
- **Wright, S. J. (2015).** "Coordinate descent algorithms." *Math. Program.* 151(1):3–34. — Survey: variants (cyclic, Gauss–Southwell, randomized), parallelism, convergence rates. Notes cyclic CD can fail to converge on certain pathological problems and that proximal gradient is a safer fallback — relevant for practitioners worried about cyclic CD inside IRLS on near-degenerate logistic data.
- **Fercoq, O., Richtárik, P. (2015).** "Accelerated, Parallel, and Proximal Coordinate Descent." *SIAM J. Optim.* 25(4):1997–2023. — APPROX: 1/k² rate for randomized parallel proximal CD, applicable to lasso/elastic-net but rarely chosen for GLM pathwise solving in practice (the warm-start chain dominates Nesterov-style acceleration).
- **Liu, J., Wright, S. J., Ré, C., Bittorf, V., Sridhar, S. (2015).** "An Asynchronous Parallel Stochastic Coordinate Descent Algorithm." *JMLR* 16(10):285–322. — Linear rate for async stochastic CD.
- **Moreau, T., et al. (2022).** "Benchopt: Reproducible, efficient and collaborative optimization benchmarks." *NeurIPS 2022*. arXiv:2206.13424. — Reproducible benchmarks of 17 lasso/logistic-l1 solvers across 4 datasets.
- **Michoel, T. (2016).** "Natural coordinate descent algorithm for L1-penalised regression in generalised linear models." *Computational Statistics & Data Analysis*. arXiv:1405.4225. — A direct one-level CD (no IRLS approximation) that uses a generic soft-thresholding mechanism at each coordinate; converges to the true optimum (unlike GLMNET, which the author notes "is not guaranteed to converge to the optimal solution"). Implemented as `glmnat` for logistic.
- **Trofimov, I., Genkin, A. (2016).** "Distributed Coordinate Descent for Generalized Linear Models with Regularization." arXiv:1611.02101. — Distributed feature-split CD with global line search; comparison to GLMNET, newGLMNET, BBR.

### Where the literature is divided

- **Whether the inner CD subproblem need be solved exactly.** Lee, Sun & Saunders (2014) and Yuan, Ho & Lin (2012) provide complementary inexactness criteria; Yuan et al. note newGLMNET's adaptive inner tolerance is what gives it CDN-like early speed *and* GLMNET-like late accuracy. Practical solvers (glmnet, picasso, adelie) tune inner tolerance heuristically.
- **Cyclic vs randomized vs Gauss–Southwell.** Wright (2015) and Nutini et al. (2015) show Gauss–Southwell is provably faster than cyclic in worst case; in practice cyclic + active-set + warm-start (glmnet) is hard to beat in wall-clock for path-following because of cache behavior. ECCD (2025) re-opens the cyclic-vs-block question by showing block-CD has stability problems on GLMs not present in Gaussian linear-lasso.
- **Whether to prefer SSNAL (semismooth Newton) on hard Lassos.** Li, Sun & Toh report SSNAL beats first-order solvers convincingly on difficult real-data Lassos; the glmnet/celer ecosystem has not adopted it, partly because path-following with warm starts re-uses the previous active set and reduces the marginal benefit of fast asymptotic rates.
- **Direct one-level CD-Newton vs IRLS-CD for *cheap* losses.** Yuan et al. 2012 explicitly leave open whether newGLMNET can compete with CDN for L2-loss SVM; the answer experimentally is "not in the early stage." For Poisson, the loss involves an `exp` term whose cost is in between logistic and L2, and the IRLS approach is preferred in practice.

## Recommendations

If you are choosing or implementing a solver for ℓ1/elastic-net GLMs, my recommendations, ordered:

1. **Default to the outer-IRLS / inner-CD architecture** (glmnet pattern). It dominates direct CD-Newton (CDN) on logistic, Poisson, and Cox precisely because exp/log evaluations are amortized from O(nℓ) per CD cycle to O(ℓ). Concretely use **glmnet** (R) / **adelie** (Python+R) / **biglasso** (memory-mapped R) for routine work, **celer**/**skglm** if you want dual extrapolation working sets in Python, **snpnet** for biobank-scale genetic data.

2. **For very large dense logistic-regression problems** (n_features dense, ℓ moderate-to-large): use newGLMNET (LIBLINEAR's `-s 6` solver since 2012) or adelie. Both handle the dense exp/log bottleneck better than glmnet, which can stall at strict tolerances on dense data — a documented failure mode in Yuan, Ho & Lin (2012, Sec. 6).

3. **For Cox models with start/stop or strata**, use glmnet 4.1+ (Tay et al. 2023) or the `coxnet` extension in adelie. Direct CD-Newton on the partial likelihood is not competitive.

4. **For numerical stability** (separated, high-imbalance, or near-collinear logistic data):
   - Always include an ε ≥ 10⁻⁵ ℓ2 component (set α<1) — Friedman et al. (2010) explicit recommendation.
   - Use a Hessian regularizer ν > 0 if implementing yourself; newGLMNET uses ν=10⁻¹² but for separation-prone data 10⁻⁶ to 10⁻⁴ is safer.
   - Floor the IRLS weights w_i ≥ 10⁻⁵ and clip the working response z_i to a bounded range; this is what glmnet does internally.
   - Use warm-started **path-following** from λ_max downward — the path itself is a regularization mechanism that prevents IRLS divergence at small λ.
   - For problems with quasi-complete separation, consider Firth-corrected logistic regression as a complement (the regularization path already mitigates non-existence of the MLE but does not fix IRLS *numerical* failure at very small λ).

5. **If your benchmarks show poor performance**, before switching architecture try in order: (a) strong rules / Gap-Safe screening, (b) working sets (celer-style), (c) `relax = TRUE` debiasing, (d) random or Gauss–Southwell coordinate selection in the inner CD, (e) only then consider SSNAL (Li, Sun & Toh 2018) for hard Lasso instances or proximal-gradient FISTA as a robustness fallback (Wright 2015 explicitly recommends keeping a PG fallback for cases where cyclic CD fails to converge).

6. **For research / new methods**: the most active 2024–2026 frontier is (i) GPU/SIMD acceleration of the inner CD recurrence (ECCD, Wang et al. 2025), (ii) block-coordinate Newton with adaptive bisection (adelie, Yang–Hastie 2024) and (iii) screening + working-set machinery (celer, biglasso). Direct one-level CD-Newton on the original GLM loss has been almost entirely abandoned outside LIBLINEAR's CDN for L2-loss SVM.

**Benchmarks/thresholds that would change the recommendation.** Switch to direct CD-Newton (CDN) only if (i) the loss has cheap derivatives (L2-SVM-like), or (ii) you have very sparse data with extremely small active sets where the per-step exp/log cost is dominated by O(nnz) and amortization gives little advantage. Switch to SSNAL or proximal-gradient when (i) the problem is a single fixed-λ Lasso (no path), (ii) the design matrix has high coherence yielding cyclic-CD oscillation, or (iii) you need certifiable duality gaps below 10⁻¹⁰.

## Caveats

- **Naming inconsistency.** "Direct Newton" / "exact Newton" / "Newton–Raphson" within CD has been used in the literature to mean two different things: (a) a *one-dimensional* Newton step on a single coordinate of the original GLM loss (CDN, Yuan et al. 2010), and (b) a *full proximal Newton* iteration whose subproblem is then solved by CD (GLMNET, newGLMNET). Most "direct Newton CD" terminology refers to (a); GLMNET is, in the Lee–Sun–Saunders sense, also a Newton-type method but the inner solver is CD on a quadratic, not a direct Newton on the GLM loss.
- **Convergence guarantees of the original GLMNET.** Friedman, Hastie, and Tibshirani (2010) do not prove convergence of the two-level scheme; they invoke Tseng (2001) for the inner CD only. Yuan, Ho & Lin (2012) explicitly state "GLMNET … its theoretical convergence is not guaranteed" and provide the first such proof for newGLMNET, but only under the assumption that the inner subproblem is solved exactly. In practice glmnet uses an adaptive inner tolerance and is not known to fail on convex GLMs, but a formal global-convergence guarantee for the actual implemented algorithm with inexact inner solves remains a gap.
- **Empirical comparisons depend strongly on tolerance, data, and hardware.** Benchopt (2022) emphasizes that "the best algorithm largely depends on the dataset properties (size, sparsity), the hyperparameters, as well as hardware." Speedup numbers cited above (10× newGLMNET vs CDN on epsilon, 3–10× adelie vs next-fastest, 2–3.8× biglasso vs glmnet/ncvreg, up to 100× celer vs scikit-learn on large lasso) are reported by the respective authors and reflect their preferred regimes.
- **Separated-data behavior under regularization.** It is widely repeated but rarely formally analyzed that the ℓ1 penalty "fixes" separation. The penalty fixes existence of the minimizer but does not fix IRLS weight degeneracy; this is a practical, implementation-level issue handled by weight flooring inside glmnet rather than by the algorithm class per se.
- **GPU coverage is thin.** Genuine GPU-accelerated coordinate descent for elastic-net GLMs has only a few entries (GENRE, ECCD, torch-gel for group lasso). Cyclic CD's inherent sequentiality conflicts with GPU parallelism; most "GPU lasso" work uses ADMM or proximal gradient. ECCD (2025) is the most promising published attempt to make the IRLS/CD architecture parallelism-friendly.
- **The Cox-specific work** of Simon et al. (2011) replaces the partial-likelihood Hessian with its diagonal — an approximation that is empirically well-behaved but is *not* an exact Newton step; this is sometimes called a "diagonalized IRLS." A direct exact-Newton CD for Cox has, to my knowledge, not been published.
